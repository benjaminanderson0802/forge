# Improving the 9x12 Postcard Method

Sep 28, 2026 · @Benjamin Anderson

## Bottom line

The 9x12 card rarely fails on mailing or design. It fails on filling the card and getting advertisers to come back. Operators on forums estimate 8 in 10 who start this business quit. Both are exactly what an autonomous loop does better than a person: tireless follow-up, proof for every advertiser, and a scheduled rebooking. The fixes that matter most, which the loop below builds in:

1. **Sell a cornerstone first.** Land one well-known local business, even free, and show its name on a "spots reserved" mockup. Then sell every other spot against that proof.
2. **Start smaller, then grow.** Open with a 6x11 community card (16 spots at $250, about 2,500 homes, about 2 weeks to fill). Move to 9x12 at $500+ once you have repeat advertisers.
3. **Prove results for every advertiser.** Give each ad its own QR code, call-tracking number or offer code, and send a results report before the next card sells. This is what drives renewals.
4. **Sell the same routes on a schedule.** Mail the same 7–8 best routes every 4–8 weeks, and sell 3-drop packages instead of one-off spots.
5. **Follow up relentlessly.** Contact 65–100 businesses per card, with follow-ups at 24 hours, 48 hours and 1 week. Most deals close on a follow-up.

About the sources: Reddit blocked my tools, and YouTube videos could only be read through summaries. So this draws on operator forum threads (Warrior Forum, mostly 2011–2018, when this business peaked), 2026 operator guides, a video summary, and 2025–2026 direct-mail benchmarks.

What worked for the operators who lasted:

- **Fewer, bigger ads.** 4–6 advertisers per card, or a smaller format.
- **A regular schedule.** One operator reported about "$1,500 a week" from one 6-ad card weekly, on a two-month renewal cycle.
- **Proof advertisers can see.** A painter got 43 calls in 14 days from a single 5,000-piece drop. Stories like that sell the next card.

One caution on the pitch: never promise a number of customers. Direct mail to cold households averages a 2–4.4% response across all offers (ANA), and a single ad among 12–16 on a card gets only a share of that.

## Unit economics: one 5,000-home drop

A 12-spot 9x12 card to 5,000 homes breaks even at about 6 spots sold (5.3 at cost). Most of the profit is in the last few spots.

Costs, from the [9x12tools worked example](https://www.9x12tools.com/9x12-method): printing about $1,209, plus postage of $1,300 (5,000 × $0.26 EDDM Retail, effective July 12, 2026). Total: about $2,509. Spots are priced at the surveyed median of $477.

| Spots sold (of 12) | Revenue | Cost | Profit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | $2,862 | $2,509 | $353 |
| 8 | $3,816 | $2,509 | $1,307 |
| 10 | $4,770 | $2,509 | $2,261 |
| 12 | $5,724 | $2,509 | $3,215 |

The smaller starter card: 16 spots × $250 = $4,000, minus $1,449 for printing and mailing to 2,500 homes, is $2,551 profit if full ([9x12 Method](https://9x12method.com/community-card-business-guide/)).

Not included above:

- tracking tools (a dynamic QR service and call-tracking numbers, billed monthly)
- payment processing fees (roughly 3%, approximate)
- your time selling

EDDM Retail allows up to 5,000 pieces per ZIP code per day, so bigger drops use a full-service printer who brings them to the postal bulk-mail center.

## The autonomous version: a card loop that runs and improves itself

Run this as a machine, not a business you operate. Forge runs every card end to end, on a fixed cadence, in as many territories as the numbers support. Every card changes one variable, and the results decide the next card. You only take calls with hesitant prospects the system scores as worth it.

**How each stage runs without you**

| Stage | What runs autonomously | Decision rule, no approval needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find territory | Scores EDDM routes by income, age and residential count; checks for competing co-op mailers already there | Launch in the highest-scoring territory that isn't saturated |
| Prospect list | Finds businesses in winning categories and flags those already advertising | Contact the top 100 per card |
| Outreach | Cold email, local Facebook group posts, and follow-ups at 24h, 48h and 1 week | Pause any message variant whose reply rate falls below the current best |
| Replies | An AI agent answers every email and inbound call: pricing, spot sizes, examples, objections. An AI receptionist on the business line is allowed because the caller calls you | Close in writing whenever the prospect agrees |
| Close + collect | Sends the agreement and Stripe payment link, confirms category exclusivity, and updates the live mockup | A spot counts as sold only once paid |
| Your call | Only for prospects who are hesitant but score high on fit and budget, with a one-page brief ready | Queue only if expected value exceeds a set threshold (e.g. a likely 3-drop package) |
| Design | Builds each ad from the logo and offer, adds its own QR code and tracking number, and sends the proof to the advertiser | The advertiser approves their own ad, not you. No reply in 48h means the proof is auto-approved, per the agreement |
| Print + mail | Orders from a full-service EDDM printer and files the mailing receipt | Print if paid spots cover costs plus the target margin by the deadline. Otherwise shrink the route count to fit, or refund and roll the spots to the next card |
| Measure + renew | Collects scans, calls and codes, then sends each advertiser a results table and a renewal offer | Auto-offer a 3-drop package to anyone above the median cost-per-response |
| Learn + adjust | Updates the experiment ledger and makes winning variants the new defaults | Promote a change only after it wins on at least 2 cards |

**How it improves itself.** Each card is one experiment. It changes one variable, and the Learner compares results against the running baseline.

- **Variables tested:** spot price, spot sizes and layout, email subject and offer, category mix, route choice, drop timing and the renewal package.
- **Metrics tracked:** close rate per 100 contacts, fill time, cost per response for advertisers, renewal rate and profit per card.
- **Scaling:** a territory with two consecutive cards filled and renewal above 40% gets cloned to the next-best adjacent territory, so cards run in parallel on staggered schedules.
- **Killing:** a territory that fails to fill twice is retired, and its lessons are recorded. A category that underdelivers for advertisers is dropped from future cards.

**Hard stops (from the Authority Charter), not approvals:**

- **Print budget cap.** Printing is only ordered from money already collected, so there is no cash at risk.
- **No outbound AI voice calls or automated texts to cold prospects.** The FCC ruled in 2024 that AI-generated voices count as "artificial" under the TCPA, which requires prior consent ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-confirms-tcpa-applies-ai-technologies-generate-human-voices)). Outbound calls stay with you, and only for the leads worth it.
- **Ad screening.** No misleading claims; licensed trades must show a license number.

The acceptance tests for each card are automatic:

- every spot paid before printing
- every proof approved by its advertiser
- mailing receipt on file
- a results report sent to every advertiser within 21 days

The number that matters most is renewal rate. No public guide reports one, so the loop measures it from card one and optimizes toward it.
